# Book v2: a situation-led, picture-rich German course on paper

Written 2026-10-26, after reading the printed A1 book (173 pp, generated from
the quizzes) and the A1.1 and A1.2 workbooks (hand-authored spreads) page by
page. This plan replaces `book_plan.md` (2026-10-18) for the printed books; the
QR system (§4 there) and the KDP print specification (§5 there) stay as they are.

## 1. Why a new book

The two books we have are each half of a good one.

| | Printed A1 book | A1.1 / A1.2 workbooks |
|---|---|---|
| Organised by | grammar unit (= quiz), 30 per part | topic spread, 16–19 per part |
| Pictures | one photo per chapter, icons | diagrams, no photos |
| Reading | two-line "in context" box | none |
| Practice | answers printed under the exercise | right-hand page, key at the back |
| Production | QR codes, key, tables, word index, credits | none of these |
| Length | 173 pages, 8 pt text, full pages | 42 + 48 pages, airy |

Defects seen in the printed book: page 5 prints almost invisible (pale text
lost in print media); type too small and pages too full; answers readable at a
glance; situation names on chapters that are really grammar sequences; no text
to read; almost nothing to look at between the chapter photo and the picture
hunt.

Book v2 keeps the workbook's pedagogy (teach left, write right, teach before
asking, diagrams, "I can") and the printed book's production (QR, key, index,
credits), and adds what neither has: words first, real photographs, reading.

## 2. The product

- **One book per sub-level**: *Deutsch A1.1* and *Deutsch A1.2*, each about
  110 pages, A4, colour. Not one 173-page A1 volume.
- **Twelve chapters per book**, one situation each, nine pages each, plus front
  matter (6 pp), two reviews (2 pp each), the back (key, tables, word index,
  picture credits, listening index: about 14 pp).
- Type never below 10 pt in body text; margins as KDP requires; answers only in
  the key.

## 3. The chapter, page by page

Every chapter has the same nine pages, so the reader always knows where they
are.

| Page | What is on it |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Words first.** The 20–30 words the chapter needs, each with its picture, article in its colour, plural. Grouped by use ("at the stall", "paying", "asking"), English in a column that can be covered. The reader meets every word before the text that uses it. |
| 2 | **Opener.** One full-page photograph of the real place, a German caption, three German questions to answer from the photo. |
| 3 | **Reading.** A dialogue or short story, 80–150 words at A1.1, 120–200 at A1.2, with the Maya cast, illustrated by two or three photos of the things it names. Five comprehension tasks on paper. |
| 4–5 | **Grammar in the situation.** At most two rules, taught with the workbook's diagrams (slots, arrow, flow, merge, build, clock, timeline) and one photo where it carries the point (a price board for numbers, a station sign for *wo*). The full tables live in the back. |
| 6 | **Real-world reading.** A photograph of an actual German document: a menu, a price board, a timetable, a ticket, a notice, a flat advert. Three tasks: find, decide, answer. |
| 7–8 | **Practise.** The workbook's exercise kinds (gaps, circle, match, sort, tags, write, clocks), picture-based wherever possible; an "about you" exercise; a Listen exercise with its QR code. No answers on the page, upside down or otherwise: the key is at the back. |
| 9 | **Listen, speak, I can.** The chapter's speaking prompt with its QR code, the listening transcript's code, four *I can* lines, and a two-line "in the app" pointer. |

Pictures per chapter: the opener, two or three photos in the reading, one
document, one or two in the grammar pages, and the word pictures. Ten to
fifteen photographs, so 120–180 per book. Every photo gets a short German
caption: reading practice that costs nothing.

## 4. The chapters, each with a world of its own

A chapter is not only a situation and two rules: it has a **world**, one real
place or theme that every photo, caption, reading and fact on its nine pages
belongs to. The world gives the chapter a face, lets the pictures be chosen
with a purpose, and carries the culture a textbook usually leaves out. Each
chapter also carries three **Wusstest du?** margin facts and one **cultural
twist** in the reading.

## 4a. Design: a book you want to pick up

The look is the site's: calm editorial, ink-navy and terracotta on warm paper,
Source Serif 4 for display, Inter for text. On paper it gets more room and more
character.

- **One colour per chapter.** Each chapter takes an accent from the palette
  (terracotta, mustard, sage, soft blue) for its page edge tab, its headings,
  its photo captions and its word page. Flicking the book, the chapters show as
  coloured bands on the fore-edge.
- **Full-bleed openers.** The opener photo bleeds off the page, with the
  chapter number large in the accent colour and the German caption set over
  the paper margin, not over the photo.
- **A grid that breathes.** Two columns on text pages, 11 pt body, 1.45
  leading, 18 mm outer margin for notes. Never more than one exercise kind per
  half page. Empty space is a feature.
- **Margin notes.** *Wusstest du?* facts, the trap of the page and the "in the
  app" pointer sit in the outer margin in small type with an icon, never in
  the text column.
- **Photos treated as photos.** Colour, not duotone; a thin ink-navy keyline;
  caption in German below, credit in the back. Documents (menus, boards) print
  as large as the page allows so they can actually be read.
- **The word page as a picture dictionary.** A 4 × 6 grid of card pictures
  with the German in the article's colour and the English in grey underneath.
  It is the page a learner photographs with their phone, so it must work alone.
- **Diagrams in the house style.** The workbook's slots, arrows and flows in
  ink-navy with the chapter accent as the highlight.
- **The cast.** Maya and the People appear in the readings as the flat scene
  illustrations, one per reading, so the book has faces that recur.
- **Chapter end.** The *I can* box and the QR codes on a half-page with the
  accent colour, so finishing a chapter looks like finishing something.
- **Cover.** The full-bleed opener of chapter 3 (market) for A1.1 and
  chapter 7 (bicycles) for A1.2, the title in the serif, the level large.

## 5. Pictures: public, real, chosen by hand

- **Source**: Wikimedia Commons, licence filter public domain, CC0, CC BY and
  CC BY-SA. Commons holds the German everyday: bakery counters, market stalls,
  Döner stands, U-Bahn platforms and signs, Deutsche Bahn boards, Spätis,
  Christmas markets, flat adverts on boards, Bürgeramt waiting rooms, flea
  markets, menus and price boards. The photos teach the culture as a side
  effect.
- **Tool** `tool/commons-pick.mjs`: search by keyword with the licence filter,
  write a contact sheet of the first 30 candidates, let GDI pick by number,
  download the original, record author, licence, title and link into
  `assets/content/photos/<book>.json`, and size it for print (300 dpi at the
  printed width). The credits page prints from that file, as the current
  book's credits already do from `_photos.json`.
- **Rules**: a person's face only in crowd scale or with a CC licence that
  allows it; no logos as the subject; SA licences noted as such in the
  credits; every photo captioned in German.
- **Word pictures** come from the app's card pictures (`static/img/<word>.webp`,
  now about a thousand). The 2026-10-21 judgment that they do not work on
  paper gets one print test on the sample chapter; the flat outlined style
  prints well in principle. Where a word has no picture, draw it through the
  companion manifest as today.

## 6. Reading texts

- One per chapter, by the chapter's situation, in the Maya cast where it fits
  (Maya at the market, Maya's first flat viewing). Not a test: a scene with a
  small twist, the fun-not-lesson rule.
- Length by level: A1.1 80–150 words, A1.2 120–200. Only words from the
  chapter's word page and earlier chapters (the companion test's "teach before
  asking" rule extends to the readings).
- The real-world reading is a photo, not typed text: the task is to read
  German as it is actually printed.
- Every reading is recorded (the audio pipeline) and carries a QR code.

## 7. Data and generator

- Content lives in `assets/content/book/<course>/<level>.json`: per chapter,
  the word list, the opener photo id, the reading (text, translation,
  questions), the two grammar blocks (reusing the companion block kinds), the
  document photo and its tasks, the practice exercises (companion exercise
  kinds), the listen and speak quiz ids, the *I can* lines.
- The page `/course/<id>/book2/<level>` renders it with the companion's
  components plus three new blocks: `wordpage`, `photo` (with caption and
  credit) and `reading`. Print through the existing `gen-books.mjs` path.
- Tests: the companion tests (answers for every exercise, taught before asked,
  gender colours, codes registered, pictures on disk) run on book v2 files
  unchanged; two new ones: every chapter has its nine pages, every photo has a
  credit.
- The grammar reference at the back is generated from the quizzes, which is
  what the current printed book does well. Nothing else of it is kept.

## 8. The work, in order

1. **Sample chapter**: *Auf dem Markt* (A1.1, chapter 3) in the full shape,
   with photos picked from Commons on a contact sheet, a reading, a price-board
   photo, the word page from card pictures, practice from the existing spread.
   Printed as a PDF and on paper. Decision point: does the format earn a book?
2. **Tool and blocks**: `commons-pick.mjs`, the three new block kinds, the
   `book2` route, the tests.
3. **A1.1**: twelve chapters, photos picked chapter by chapter, readings
   written and recorded, reviews, back matter. Print proof, fit check on every
   page (free-space for practice pages, the learn-fit check for the rest).
4. **A1.2** the same way.
5. **Covers** at size 2048, KDP upload of both, the home-page offer updated to
   the two books, the companion workbooks withdrawn from the shelf once the
   books are out.
6. A2 and up follow the same pattern, one sub-level at a time, after A1 has
   been in readers' hands.

## 9. Open decisions

- Whether the current 173-page A1 book still goes to the test readers, or the
  sample chapter does. My view: send the sample chapter with two or three
  existing spreads, and ask which they would rather learn from.
- Card pictures on paper: decided by the sample chapter's print test.
- Whether a chapter may run to ten pages when the situation is rich (market,
  doctor). My view: yes, never more.
