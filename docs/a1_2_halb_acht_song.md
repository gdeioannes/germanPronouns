# HALB ACHT – Max's second date (the A1.2 "what time?" rap)

The sequel to "Wie bitte?". Lena asked for a second date, and Max now has a new problem: German clocks.
One story, three appointments, each missed in a new way. First the classic trap: "halb acht" is
half *to* eight (7:30), not half past. Then Max is on time ("Viertel nach drei") at the wrong one of
Berlin's many stations, so "Ich verstehe nur Bahnhof" finally comes true. Then he's very much on time
for Lena's mum's birthday "um sieben"… at seven in the morning. The outro shows he's learned the
clock: he's early, and it's the wrong day.

## What the song teaches (for the song page's `teaches` list)

- halb acht = 7:30 (half to the next hour, not half past)
- Viertel nach drei = 3:15 / es ist halb neun = it's 8:30
- Um wie viel Uhr? / Wann? → um halb acht, um sieben
- am Samstag, am Sonntag, am zehnten Oktober, am Abend
- Hast du am … Zeit? / Am … kann ich nicht.
- Wo bist du? / Welcher …?

## Notes

- One story, in order: date one missed (time) → reschedule → date two missed (place) → invited to
  the birthday → date three hit, at the wrong half of the day → date four: he's learned the clock, and gets the day wrong.
- What happens next shows the meaning: the empty cinema shows "halb acht" isn't 8:30, the pyjamas show
  "um sieben" can mean 7 a.m., Lena's "Heute ist Sonntag" shows he got the day wrong. English echoes
  only where the story can't show it ("Seven thirty!", "Who's this?", "Today is Sunday.").
- Callbacks to "Wie bitte?": "Noch einmal?" set up this date, and "Ich verstehe nur Bahnhof" comes back as real stations.
- The mum's line plays on the German punctuality cliché: the one time he's way too early is the time it wins.
- Lena and her family use du with Max; nobody needs Sie here.
- No exam names and no level talk in the lyrics.
